Exam 1 mainly focuses on four skills: Shifting supply and demand, reading budget constraints, working out opportunity cost and comparative advantage, and explaining each step in words as well as interpreting them. 

ALMOST ALL POINTS GO INTO EXPLANATION

Supply and demand shifters

| Shifter                            | Curve        | Example (Jelly beans)                  |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Input Costs rise                   | Supply left  | Sugar price rises                      |
| More firms enter                   | Supply right | Willy Wonka enters the market          |
| Better tech                        | Supply right | Faster jelly bean sorter               |
| Price of substitute rises          | Demand right | Bubble gum gets prices                 |
| Tastes/Preferences fall            | Demand left  | A research links jelly beans to cancer |
| Income falls                       | Demand left  | Recession                              |
| Buyers expect higher future prices | Demand right | Buy now before prices rise             |
| Population grows                   | Demand right | More buyers                            |

IMPORTANT TO REMEMBER!! Demand shifts moves P and Q the same way. Supply shifts move them in opposite ways

Double Shifts
- When both curves shift, one of P or Q is certain and the other is indeterminate. Which one depends on the sizes of the shifts.

| Supply | Demand | Price | Quantity |
| ------ | ------ | ----- | -------- |
| Right  | Right  | ?     | Up       |
| Left   | Left   | ?     | Down     |
| Right  | Left   | Down  | ?        |
| Left   | Right  | Up    | ?        |

SI Slides House-Price Question: 
1. Which of the following would be most likely to increase the price of a new house?
a. Lower wages for carpenters, lower wood prices, decreases in consumer incomes, lower apartment rents, decreases in population, and buyers' expectations of lower house prices in the future.

b. Lower wages for carpenters, lower wood prices, increases in consumer incomes, higher apartment rents, increases in population and expectations of higher house prices in the future.

c. Lower wages for carpenters, higher wood prices, decreases in consumer incomes, higher apartment rents, decreases in population and expectations of higher house prices in the future.

d. Higher wages for carpenters, higher wood prices, increases in consumer incomes,
higher apartment rents, increases in population and expectations of higher house prices in the future.

> [!note]- Answer
> The answer is D. As Higher carpenter wages and wood prices shift supply left. Higher incomes, higher apartment rents (a substitute), population growth and expected price increases all shift demand right. Supply left plus demand right means price definitely rises!


How a market moves to a new Equilibrium
This is the "Transition" sentence exams ask for. At the old price after a shift, there is a shortage ($Q_d > Q_s$) or a surplus ($Q_s > Q_d$). 
- Shortage: Buyers compete for too few units and bid the price up. $Q_s$ rises along supply and $Q_d$ falls along demand until they meet.
- Surplus: Sellers compete to unload extra units and cut the price. $Q_d$ rises and $Q_s$ falls until they meet

Absolute vs. Comparative Advantage
- Absolute Advantage: you produce more with the same resources, or use fewer resources for the same output.
- Comparative Advantage: You have the lower opportunity cost, meaning you give up less of the other good.
- One part can have an absolute advantage in both goods, but never a comparative advantage in both.
- Workflow: output per hour -> absolute advantage. Total output -> opportunity costs -> comparative advantage. Opportunity costs -> range of trade prices.
- For output, a higher number is better. For opportunity cost, a lower number is cheaper.

Worker SI problems
Orange juice (Strom season ends, more oranges): The input (oranges) gets cheaper, so supply shifts right and P falls while Q rises. At the old price there was a surplus, so sellers cut prices and buyers purchase more until the new equilibrium. If Q falls instead: a new storm destroys orange crops, supply shifts left, and P rises while Q falls.

Riveradable Bakery (D: P = 50 - Q, S: P = 20 + Q):
- Equilibrium: 50 - Q = 20 + Q, so Q* = 15 loaves and P* = $35
