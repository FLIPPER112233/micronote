[[Vocabulary/Opportunity Cost\|Opportunity Cost]] - Highest valued, next-best alternative that you have to give up to attain something

#### Example
If a team member spent 6 minutes building, what could they have done instead?
- "We chose to **have more builders than throwers**"
- "The next-best alternative was to **have more throwers than builders**."
- "Therefore, the opportunity cost was **the extra practice throws/technique refinement we could have gotten with more throwers.**"


[[Vocabulary/Marginal Benefit\|Marginal Benefit]] = Benefit that arises from a small incremental change in activity 
[[Vocabulary/Marginal Cost\|Marginal Cost]] = cost that arises from a small incremental change in activity

### Chapter 1 

As a society, people face other trade-offs to get what they want. There are many different types of trade-offs.

#### Societal Trade-Offs:
[[Vocabulary/Efficiency\|Efficiency]] - the property of society getting the most it can from its scarce resources

[[Vocabulary/Equality\|Equality]]- the property of distributing economic prosperity uniformly among the members of society

Since people face trade-offs, they need to compare the costs and benefits of alternative decisions. 
- Opportunity cost is an example that people consider

Economists often assume that people are rational

[[Vocabulary/Rational People\|Rational People]] - people who systematically and purposefully do the best they can to achieve their objectives
- Firms tend to decide how many workers to hire and how much product to make and sell to maximize profits. 

[[Vocabulary/Marginal Change\|Marginal Change]] - Economists use this term to describe an incremental adjustment to n existing plan of action
- Margin means edge
- So marginal changes are small adjustments around the edges of what you are doing

[[Vocabulary/Rational People\|Rational People]] make decisions by comparing [[Vocabulary/Marginal Cost\|Marginal Cost]] and [[Vocabulary/Marginal Benefit\|Marginal Benefit]]

Example: Suppose you are deciding whether to watch a movie tonight. You pay $30 a month for a streaming service that gives you unlimited access to a library, and you would watch around 5 movies a month. 
- What cost should you consider when deciding whether to stream another movie?
	- You might think 30/5 which makes the average cost of the movie
- But think about marginal cost... it would be 0 since you already paid the $30 regardless of how many movies you watch.
	- At that margin, streaming a movie is free.
	- Only cost is time.

[[Vocabulary/Incentive\|Incentive]] - Something that induces a person to act, such as the prospect of a punishment or reward
- People respond to incentives if they make decisions by comparing costs and benefits. 

**<font color="#9bbb59">Trade benefits everyone</font>**. As trade allows countries to specialize in what they do best and to enjoy a greater variety of goods and services.

[[Vocabulary/Market Economy\|Market Economy]] - An economy that allocates resources through the decentralized decisions of many firms and households as they interact in markets for goods and services 


A reason why we need government is that the invisible hand can work its magic only if government enforces the rules and maintains the institutions that are key to a market economy. 
- Market economies need institutions to enforce [[Vocabulary/Property rights\|Property rights]] to individuals can own and control scarce resources. 
The government has this invisible hand, which is powerful, but not omnipotent.
- Two broad rationales for a government to intervene
	1. To promote efficiency
	2. To promote equality

Sometimes the invisible hand does not work out in the run having [[Vocabulary/Market failure\|Market failure]]
- This usually happens when the market does not produce an efficient allocation of resources on its own.
	- One possible cause of market failure is an [[Vocabulary/Externality\|Externality]], which is the impact of one person's actions on the well-being of a bystander
		- Example would be pollution. When the production of a good pollutes the air and creates health problems for those whose live near the factories the market may fail to take this into account
	- Another reason is [[Vocabulary/Market power\|Market power]], which refers to the ability of a single person or firm to unduly influence market prices.
		- Example would be everyone in town needs water but there is only one well, the owner of the well does not face competition with which the invisible hand normally keeps self-interest in check; the owner may take advantage of this opportunity by restricting the output of water and charging a higher price. In the presence of externalities or market power, well-designed public policy can enhance efficiency.

[[Vocabulary/Productivity\|Productivity]] - The quantity of goods and services produced from each unit of labor input

[[Vocabulary/Inflation\|Inflation]] - An increase in the overall level of prices in the economy
- <font color="#9bbb59">What causes inflation? </font>
	- When a government creates large quantities of the nation's money, the value of the money falls which causes prices of goods to rise.

[[Vocabulary/Business Cycle\|Business Cycle]] - Fluctuations in economic activity, such as employment and production
