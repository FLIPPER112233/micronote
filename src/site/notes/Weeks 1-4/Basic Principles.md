### Economics

Scarcity -> Choice -> Tradoffs (Costs)

[[Vocabulary/Opportunity Cost\|Opportunity Cost]] - Highest valued, next-best alternative that you have to give up to attain something

#### Example
If a team member spent 6 minutes building, what could they have done instead?
- "We chose to **have more builders than throwers**"
- "The next-best alternative was to **have more throwers than builders**."
- "Therefore, the opportunity cost was **the extra practice throws/technique refinement we could have gotten with more throwers.**"


[[Vocabulary/Marginal Benefit\|Marginal Benefit]] = Benefit that arises from a small incremental change in activity 
[[Vocabulary/Marginal Cost\|Marginal Cost]] = cost that arises from a small incremental change in activity 

BIG PICTURE
1. Scarcity - Resources are limited
2. Opportunity Cost - Choices mean giving up the next best alternative 
3. Marginal Thinking - Compare the additional benefit with the additional cost
4. Make choices that improve your outcome

### Chapter 1 

As a society, people face other trade-offs to get what they want. There are many different types of trade-offs.


#### Societal Trade-Offs:
[[Vocabulary/Efficiency\|Efficiency]] - the property of society getting the most it can from its scarce resources

[[Vocabulary/Equality\|Equality]]- the property of distributing economic prosperity uniformly among the members of society

Since people face trade-offs, they need to compare the costs and benefits of alternative decisions. 
- Opportunity cost is an example that people consider

Economists often assume that people are rational

[[Vocabulary/Rational People\|Rational People]] - people who systematically and purposefully do the best they can to achieve their objectives
