> [!info]-
> Because resources are scarce, every choice involves a tradeoff. The production possibilities frontier (PPF), or a budget constraint for an individual, shows the maximum combinations of two goods that can be made or bought with fixed resources, technology, income, and prices. Points on the curve are productively efficient, meaning nothing is wasted. Points inside are inefficient, which happens when resources are idle, and points outside are unattainable, which shows scarcity. Only one point on the curve is allocatively efficient: the mix society wants most. The slope of the curve is the opportunity cost, what you give up divided by what you gain. A straight line means constant opportunity cost. A bowed-out curve reflects the law of increasing opportunity cost, because resources aren't equally suited to producing both goods. The curve shifts outward with more resources or better technology and inward with losses like disasters. It pivots when a change affects only one good, such as a new technology or a price change on a budget constraint. Unemployment doesn't shift the curve at all; it just moves the economy to a point inside it.

### Model: Circular-Flow Diagram
- This model shows, the economy includes only two types of decision makers, firms and households. 
	- Firms produce goods and services using inputs such as labor,, land, and capital (buildings and machines)
		- These inputs are called the factors of production
	- Households own the factors of production and consume all goods and services that the firms produce.

The interact in two types of markets. 
- In the markets for goods and services
	- Households are buyers, and firms are sellers
		- Households buy the output of goods and services that firms produce
- In the Markets for the factors of production
	- Households are sellers, and firms are buyers. 
		- In these markets, households provide the inputs that firms use to produce goods and services. 

The [[Vocabulary/Circular-Flow Diagram\|Circular-Flow Diagram]] offers a simple way of organizing all the transactions between households and firms in an economy
- Two loops of the circular-flow diagram are distinct but related.
	- The inner loop represents the flows of inputs and outputs
		- Households sell the use of their labor, land, and capital to firms. Firms will then produce goods to sell to households
	- The outer loop of the diagram represents the corresponding flow of dollars
		- Households spend money to buy goods and services from firms. Firms will use some of the revenue from these sales to purchase the factors of production, like paying wages.

Shifts in the Production Possibilities Frontier
Outward Shift (Economic Growth)
- Increase in Resource Quantity
- Improvement in Resource Quality (Like better Education and worker training) = more productive
- Technological Advancements
Inward Shift (Economic Decline)
- Loss of Resources
